# Timeline Alignment Principles

This project is shaped by a private, full-corpus review of the approved X
archive. The archive remains local. This document publishes the recurring
principles without publishing private post text.

## Core Purpose

Build timelines that let people inspect how the present emerged. A timeline
should make scattered evidence understandable without pretending to become a
single unquestionable authority.

## Product Principles

1. **Trace the trail.** Every public event should connect a date, a narrow claim,
   and inspectable sources.
2. **Compress without flattening.** Summaries should make large stories legible
   while preserving links, disputes, uncertainty, and interpretation.
3. **Separate layers of truth.** Distinguish observed facts, official framing,
   reporting, commentary, personal observation, and unresolved questions.
4. **Preserve context.** Events should support relationships, related events,
   expandable detail, and multiple views instead of becoming isolated cards.
5. **Let design carry function.** Readability, visual organization, sharing,
   and navigation are part of the evidence system, not decoration applied later.
6. **Support parallel thinking.** A useful interface should allow chronology,
   themes, sources, and cross-connections to coexist without forcing everything
   into one folder hierarchy.
7. **Make tools interoperable.** The data model should be portable enough for
   websites, archives, research tools, and future agents to consume.
8. **Keep humans in judgment loops.** Automation may extract and propose.
   Reviewers decide what becomes public, especially for safety-sensitive or
   interpretive claims.
9. **Treat unresolved context honestly.** A linked reaction, partial archive,
   or ambiguous claim should remain visibly incomplete until its context is
   recovered.
10. **Document transitions, not only launches.** Track how technology changes
    work, culture, institutions, public understanding, and everyday life.
11. **Prefer lived usefulness over spectacle.** The best timeline helps people
    understand what changed, what remains unreliable, and what choices follow.
12. **Keep the human scale visible.** Progress stories should retain questions
    of wellbeing, access, meaning, creativity, and public response.

## Interface Direction

The presentation layer should feel editorial, inspectable, and calm:

- year-grouped chronology with expandable records
- clear source labels and evidence status
- related-event paths and thematic views
- readable typography and restrained visual hierarchy
- public summaries with deeper detail available on demand

## Review Boundary

Private archives can inform the project without being published. Verbatim source
material stays local unless explicitly approved. Public artifacts should contain
reviewed principles, narrow proposals, and cited evidence.
